Worm Gear Motor: Applications, Advantages and Selection Guide

How worm gear motors work—and how to select power, ratio, torque, mounting and electrical supply

Published August 27, 2026 · Reviewed by SMK Transmission

Quick answer: A worm gear motor combines an electric motor with a worm gearbox to reduce speed and increase output torque. Its compact right-angle design, smooth operation and broad ratio range make it useful for conveyors, packaging machinery, food-processing equipment and industrial automation.

For applications requiring low speed, high torque and limited installation space, an NMRV worm gear motor is often a practical and economical drive solution.

What Is a Worm Gear Motor?

A worm gear motor mainly consists of an electric motor, worm shaft and worm wheel. The motor drives the worm shaft, which transfers power to the worm wheel at a 90-degree angle. This transmission reduces motor speed while increasing the available output torque.

Motor speed: 1400 rpm
Gear ratio: 30:1
Theoretical output speed: approximately 47 rpm

Actual output speed can vary depending on motor speed, load and gearbox efficiency.

Main Advantages of Worm Gear Motors

1. Compact 90° Design

The input and output shafts are arranged at right angles, making worm gear motors suitable for machines where installation space is limited.

2. High Reduction Ratio

A single-stage NMRV worm gearbox can provide a wide range of ratios, making it easy to obtain lower output speeds without a complicated transmission structure.

3. Smooth and Quiet Operation

The sliding contact between the worm and worm wheel provides smooth transmission with relatively low vibration and noise.

4. High Output Torque

By reducing motor speed, the gearbox increases output torque, making worm geared motors suitable for conveyors, mixers, packaging machines and other industrial equipment.

5. Self-Locking Capability

Some worm gearbox configurations can provide a degree of self-locking or resistance to back-driving, particularly at higher reduction ratios. However, self-locking should not be used as a substitute for a mechanical brake in safety-critical applications.

Common Worm Gear Motor Applications

Worm gear motors are widely used in:

  • Belt and roller conveyors
  • Packaging machinery
  • Food-processing equipment
  • Mixers and agitators
  • Agricultural machinery
  • Material-handling systems
  • Textile machinery
  • Automatic gates
  • Industrial automation equipment
  • Lifting and positioning equipment

Their combination of compact size, low output speed and high torque makes them particularly useful for conveyor and automation applications.

How to Select the Right Worm Gear Motor

Before selecting a worm gear motor, confirm the following parameters.

Motor Power

Determine the required motor power according to the machine load. Common examples include 0.18, 0.37, 0.75, 1.1, 1.5 and 2.2 kW.

Output Speed

Confirm the required output rpm. The approximate relationship is:

Output speed = motor speed ÷ gear ratio

Gear Ratio

Common NMRV gearbox ratios include 7.5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 40, 50, 60, 80 and 100.

Output Torque

Motor power alone is not enough for gearbox selection. Required output torque, operating hours and load conditions should also be considered.

Mounting Position

Common configurations include hollow shaft output, single or double output shafts, output flange, foot mounting and torque arm.

Voltage and Frequency

For international projects, motor voltage and frequency should always be confirmed before ordering. Common options include 220V, 380V, 400V, 415V and 440V; 50Hz or 60Hz; single-phase or three-phase.

NMRV Worm Gear Motor

The NMRV worm gear motor is one of the most widely used configurations for industrial machinery. Common gearbox sizes include NMRV025, NMRV030, NMRV040, NMRV050, NMRV063, NMRV075, NMRV090, NMRV110, NMRV130 and NMRV150. Different motor powers, ratios, output shafts and mounting configurations can be selected according to the application.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a worm gear motor?

It combines an electric motor and worm gearbox to reduce speed, increase torque and provide right-angle power transmission.

Where are worm gear motors used?

Typical uses include conveyors, packaging machines, mixers, food-processing equipment, gates, agricultural machinery and automation systems.

How do I select a worm gear motor?

Confirm output speed and torque, motor power, ratio, duty, mounting, shaft arrangement, voltage, frequency and operating environment.

Can self-locking replace a brake?

No. Do not rely on worm gearbox self-locking as a safety brake. Safety-critical applications require an independently selected braking or holding system.

THREE-PHASE MOTOR TROUBLESHOOTING

Why Is a Three-Phase Motor Overheating?

How to identify electrical, mechanical and operating causes before the winding insulation is damaged.

Published August 17, 2026 · Reviewed by SMK Transmission

Quick answer: A three-phase motor usually overheats because it is overloaded, receives an unbalanced or incorrect supply, starts too frequently, has restricted cooling, operates outside its rated duty, or drives equipment with excessive friction. Measure all three phase voltages and currents under load before replacing the motor.

First Checks When a Motor Runs Hot

Stop the machine if there is smoke, a burning smell, repeated protection trips, abnormal noise or rapid temperature rise. After isolating the power safely, check the driven machine, coupling, gearbox, bearings, fan and air passages. When the machine can be tested, record voltage and current on all three phases instead of measuring only one conductor.

1. Mechanical Overload

If the required shaft torque is higher than the motor's continuous capability, current rises and copper losses increase. Common causes include an overloaded conveyor, blocked pump, tight bearing, incorrect gearbox ratio or a machine that requires excessive acceleration torque. Compare measured current with the nameplate current under normal production load.

2. Voltage Imbalance or Phase Loss

Three-phase motors require a balanced supply. Loose terminals, damaged contactors, undersized cables or upstream supply problems can create unequal voltages. Current imbalance is often much greater than voltage imbalance, so a motor can overheat even when the average voltage appears acceptable. Phase loss is more severe and requires immediate shutdown.

3. Incorrect Voltage, Frequency or Connection

Confirm the nameplate voltage, frequency and winding connection. A motor intended for one star/delta voltage arrangement may draw excessive current or provide insufficient torque when connected incorrectly. Frequency also affects synchronous speed and the motor's voltage-to-frequency relationship.

4. Restricted Cooling and High Ambient Temperature

A blocked fan cover, damaged fan, dust layer or insufficient clearance around the motor reduces heat transfer. Verify that the selected enclosure and insulation class suit the dust, moisture, altitude and ambient temperature of the installation.

5. Frequent Starting or Unsuitable Duty

Starting current creates significant heat. Applications with frequent starts, reversing, braking or long acceleration periods must be checked against the motor duty and permitted starts per hour. A motor sized only from steady running power may be unsuitable for this cycle.

6. Bearings, Alignment and Gearbox Condition

Worn bearings, excessive belt tension, poor coupling alignment and gearbox damage increase mechanical losses. Disconnecting the load for a controlled diagnostic test can help separate a motor problem from a driven-equipment problem.

Information to Record Before Asking for Support

  • Motor power, rated current, rated speed, voltage and frequency
  • Measured voltage and current on all three phases under load
  • Ambient temperature, enclosure and mounting arrangement
  • Starts per hour, duty cycle and acceleration time
  • Driven equipment, gearbox ratio and required output torque
  • Photos of the nameplate and wiring terminals
Selection advice: Do not automatically replace a hot motor with a larger frame. Correct the electrical or mechanical cause first, then verify the required power, torque, starting duty and service margin.

Frequently Asked Questions

What temperature is normal for a three-phase motor?

Acceptable temperature depends on insulation class, ambient conditions, measurement location and manufacturer limits. Compare winding temperature or temperature rise with the motor documentation rather than relying only on touch.

Can low voltage make a motor overheat?

Yes. When load torque remains high, low voltage can cause the motor to draw more current and operate with reduced torque margin.

Can a VFD cause motor overheating?

It can if the motor runs slowly with inadequate self-cooling, the V/f settings are wrong, the switching conditions are unsuitable, or cable and filter requirements are ignored.
